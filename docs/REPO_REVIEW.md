# Repository review and implemented update

Reviewed from the supplied source archive (original archive commit `287589b62dc55b0d86121a94fb81b6d0d41c7be0`). Work is local; no GitHub branch, pull request or deployment was created.

## Overall assessment

The game has useful foundations: content packs separate learning material from the adventure; the two language tracks can share a runtime; existing recordings, local profiles, offline support and familiar rewards are worth retaining. The main weaknesses were duplicated application logic, curriculum material without a route into practice, fragile progress migration, and exercise generation that assumed one surface form must be uniquely correct.

There is no evidence from this repository alone that combat, exploration, coins, equipment or labyrinths need to be simplified. Those mechanics remain. Their value should be judged by observing children: do they help children practise, or consume most of the session in menus and repeated unlocking? The appropriate cleanup here is in implementation and curriculum reliability.

## Danish: scope and limits

The course now has **60 units: 20 each for 0., 1. and 2. klasse**. The month-by-month goals and references are in [the curriculum map](CURRICULUM_MAP.md).

Grade 0 comprehension presents spoken content, with visual choices and buttons to hear textual answers. It teaches letter shapes and sounds without demanding independent word reading. Grades 1–2 develop spelling/dictation, supported and independent reading, and story comprehension. Vocabulary practice gradually shifts from isolated letters to words. Maths and logic extend to place value, addition/subtraction, small multiplication/division families, measurement, money/time, patterns, categorisation and simple data interpretation.

| Material | Before | After |
|---|---:|---:|
| Authored units | 14 | 60 |
| Graphemes | 25 | 25 |
| Words | 209 | 275 |
| Structured reading/listening tasks | 156 | 276 |
| Maths/logic tasks | 246 | 1,246 |

The arithmetic count includes deliberately generated number families; it should not be mistaken for 1,246 different teaching concepts. Thirty authored month stories and parent activities provide the language contexts. Writing, explaining reasoning, oral retelling and real-world measurement require the accompanying adult activities. The app therefore supports the requested subjects through grade 2, but does not replace classroom teaching or assess every Danish competence.

The two-units-per-month schedule is an editorial pacing choice. It should adjust to the child, teacher and school calendar. In particular, the grade-2 arithmetic progression and the transition from supported to independent reading deserve a Danish primary teacher's review.

## Armenian: breadth and reachability

The course extends from 25 to **49 authored levels (0–48)**. It keeps the early sequence and adds 24 units: eight A2 units and sixteen B1-oriented units. New themes include arranging visits, recounting experiences, asking for clarification, preferences, travel, solving problems, opinions with reasons, conditions, teamwork, news sources, environmental choices, traditions, feelings, service requests, summaries, uncertainty and community projects.

There are **192 new short sentence records and 24 connected passages**, with two comprehension questions per passage. Seventy-four vocabulary/phrase definitions that were not already early core material support those units; some reuse existing imported IDs. The Italian and English explanations and exercises are synchronized.

| Armenian material | Italian before | Italian after | English before | English after |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Dictionary words/phrases | 760 | 822 | 665 | 727 |
| Active words/phrases | 277 | 409 | 277 | 409 |
| Sentence/passage records | 342 | 558 | 110 | 539 |
| Active sentence/passage records | 118 | 539 | 110 | 539 |
| Connected passages added | — | 24 | — | 24 |

`activation.json` routes 58 previously unused words and 205 previously unused sentences into the active course; it also supplies eight missing English translations for existing core sentences. All remaining imported extension records are accounted for: **413 words and 19 sentences** are explicitly listed with the reason they still need review. They remain available as source material. Loading unreviewed glosses indiscriminately into quizzes would not solve the underlying content problem.

The new passages support understanding gist, detail, reasons and relations between events. Chapter missions invite retelling, expressing opinions, negotiating and summarising with an adult. The game does not evaluate spontaneous spoken interaction or written production, so these units are **practice toward B1**, not evidence that a child has attained full B1 proficiency.

## Fair answers

Correctness is centralized in `isCorrectAnswer`. Explicit alternatives are accepted with normalization of case, whitespace, Unicode composition and final punctuation. Alternative orderings are not guessed by comparing unordered word bags: moving words can change meaning. Authored order tasks declare their accepted sentences; their draggable/tappable chips do not carry terminal punctuation into the middle of a reordered sentence.

Unreviewed Armenian order/gap generation is no longer selected. Most Armenian questions present a sentence or passage and ask about meaning in the source language. Word distractors avoid identical Armenian forms, declared aliases, identical translations and duplicate visible choices. Danish gaps identify a particular word through a complete spoken source, and a sentence-order instruction requests the exact sentence heard.

A further audit found a symmetric-location distractor: “the bridge is near the station” versus “the station is near the bridge”. That distractor was replaced. Automated checks catch structural ambiguity and duplicate labels, but cannot prove all natural-language interpretations are unique. Native review must still examine target grammar, translations, plausible paraphrases and factual distractors together. Armenian target content remains marked for native-speaker review; reviewed Italian/English copy does not change that status.

## Placement and preservation

Settings → **Level and progress** offers:

1. Direct placement chosen by a parent.
2. An optional untimed check with two distinct items per learning area. It samples the preceding unit and earlier review material. At least 7/8 suggests moving to the chosen unit; it is deliberately not a B1 test or a comprehensive adaptive assessment.
3. A JSON backup of the active child's current course, with a preview before restoration. Armenian backups can move between the Italian and English versions.

Placement retains coins, inventory, XP and item practice histories, and establishes suitable combat statistics for the destination. It does not award fictitious item mastery or completed training sessions. Moving backwards caps combat statistics to the chosen level; export a backup first if you want an exact return point. Revisited encounters can be fought again.

The Danish migration maps old levels 0–9 to 0,2,…,18 and old levels 10–13 to 20,23,26,29. This approximates the earlier course's beginning-primary scope while retaining progress. It runs once, using `course_revision: 2`, and maps the corresponding per-level training and labyrinth history. Armenian levels 0–24 retain their numbers and stable IDs. Out-of-range legacy Danish progress is not reset to the beginning.

Browser storage still depends on the browser profile and site origin. Ordinary updates on the same origin preserve it; moving to a new host/device or clearing site data requires the exported backup. A backup is per child/course, not a copy of all profiles, device settings or locally contributed recordings.

## Recommendations, in priority order

1. **Review and record the new Armenian units.** A native Eastern Armenian educator should review the sentences, full passage translations, distractors and approximate romanization together. There are recordings for 323 of 539 active sentence/passage records; the 216 new records currently rely on browser speech or adult reading. Existing automated recordings also still need pronunciation review. The existing audio-generation workflow can produce candidate recordings, but listening quality must be checked on actual family devices.
2. **Pilot the grade-0 interface and grade-2 progression with a teacher and children.** Check audible choices, symbol size, replay controls, reading demands, frustration and time spent navigating. Treat the school-month mapping as adjustable. Increase authored story variety where children encounter repetition too often.
3. **Track skills separately from adventure levels.** Future reporting should distinguish reading, listening, supported answers and independent production, while keeping stable content IDs. The current word/sentence memory is useful but is not a calibrated competence measure.
4. **Add productive tasks with adult feedback.** Retelling, short messages, explanations and a few written sentences are essential beyond recognition. Use the existing chapter missions as the starting point. A conservative teacher/parent rubric is preferable to pretending multiple-choice accuracy or speech recognition certifies language ability.
5. **Finish the technical cleanup after browser verification.** The stylesheet is still roughly 7,500 lines and needs component-level ownership plus visual regression coverage. Storage implementations still overlap. Consolidate them in a separate change with migration fixtures; preserve the existing profiles and deployed storage keys. Retire obsolete phase scripts after confirming no maintainer relies on them.

See [validation results and remaining release checks](VALIDATION.md). The source changes are reviewable, but the full production build and interactive browser smoke test have not been verified in this environment.
