# Curriculum map

Units are stored and displayed with zero-based level numbers. A teaching month is approximately four school weeks; holidays do not create extra levels. A and B cover introduction and consolidation/application within the month. Placement can override the suggested pace.

Grade 0 comprehension requires no word reading. Letter recognition is taught through spoken words and letter shapes. Grades 1–2 combine dictation/spelling, reading, listening, short stories and parent activities. The course is focused on language, maths and logic rather than all six kindergarten competence areas.

## Eastern Armenian

Levels 0–8 retain the introductory sequence: alphabet and greetings; introductions; family; needs; everyday actions; places; numbers and time; plurals and negation; integrated exchanges.

Each new unit at levels 25–48 includes eight short sentence records and one connected passage with two distinct comprehension questions. Vocabulary/phrase glosses are added where they are new; known words recur in more demanding contexts.

Receptive practice uses Italian or English questions and answer choices alongside Armenian text or audio. Chapter missions invite explaining, retelling, comparing and negotiating with an adult. These productive activities are not automatically graded. B1 includes abilities that multiple-choice questions cannot establish.

The sequence is an editorial interpretation of these frameworks, not an official monthly curriculum. The kindergarten document prioritises playful language and mathematical awareness; its letter-recognition attention point excludes q, w, x and z. The Danish subject framework extends to reading, expression, interpretation and communication. The maths framework groups early-primary outcomes through grade 3, so grade-2 pacing here needs teacher review. As noted on the official Danish documents, knowledge/skill goals became advisory in 2025, while the listed competence requirements and attention points remain binding.
